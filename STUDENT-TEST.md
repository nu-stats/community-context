# Testing the community-context site (about 30 minutes)

Thank you for testing this. The site also has a "Test this site" tab with the questions below and a button that saves your answers as a text file. We want to know where a student gets stuck. Do not ask anyone for help while you work; write down what you did instead. Nothing here is graded.

You need: the site open in a browser, a pen or a notes file, and the assignment (`community-context.md`).

## Steps

1. Pick one group (country of birth) that you know something about, for example the country your family or a friend's family comes from.
2. Open the site. Choose your group, rank the cities by **2020-2024 survey**, and choose three indicators from the menu (population and foreign_born_percent are given).
3. Look at the table of 50 cities. Write down the first three cities and their group sizes in 2020-2024. Does this match what you expected about where the group lives?
4. Choose a different ranking period (for example 2000 Census). Did the first three cities change? Which cities appear at only one period?
5. In **Step 2**, type the national value of each of your three variables for each period. (The assignment says the course provides them. If you cannot find them, write that down.)
6. In **Step 3**, find the weighted average and the plain average for one variable. Write down both for each of the three periods.
7. Check one number by hand. Take the first 5 cities for one variable and one period, multiply group size by value for each, add up, and divide by the sum of the group sizes. It will not match the page (the page uses 50 cities), but you should be able to say how each row on the page is made.
8. In **Step 4**, find the share of your group that lives in these 50 cities. Write one sentence about what that means for your results.
9. Click **Download this table as CSV** and open the file in Excel or Google Sheets.
10. Write the one-sentence profile from the assignment for one variable in 2020-2024: "the average member of this group lived in cities where XX.X% ..."

## What to compare against

- The page against itself: the weighted average should be the sum of (group size x value) divided by the sum of the group sizes. Check this with a calculator for one variable and one period.
- The page against the workbook: if you have the course workbook, choose the same group, ranking period and variables there. The top cities, the sums and the weighted averages should match.
- The page against what you know: do the cities make sense for your group?

## Places you might get stuck

- **Which number is the weight?** It is the size of your group in each city, not the city's population. Say whether the page made this clear.
- **Weighted average versus plain average.** They are different, and the difference is the point of the assignment. Say whether you could tell which row is which.
- **Blank cells or a national value you cannot find.** Say what you did.
