Task 1. Choose three indicators

Two indicators are given: population (a count) and foreign_born_percent. Choose three more above, from poverty_percent, unemployment_percent, median_household_income, bachelors_percent, renter_percent, black_percent and hispanic_percent. You use the same indicators in every period. All five are shown for the 50 cities; the weighted averages are for the three you chose.

Task 2. Find the cities where most of your group lives

Open the Top 50 tab. It shows the group name and the 50 cities with the most people born in your group, ranked by the period you chose, with the size of your group in each of the three periods. To do it by hand: on the Data tab, click the group_size column for that period to sort largest to smallest. The first 50 rows are your cities.

Task 3. Collect the indicators for each city and period

The Top 50 tab also shows, for each city, the five indicators (the two given and your three) in all three periods. These are whole-city values.

Task 4. Weighted averages

The Weighted averages tab shows, for each variable and each period, the calculation step by step: the sum of the weights, the sum of weight times value, the weighted average, the plain average of the 50 cities, the national value, and the weighted average minus the plain average.

weighted average = SUM(weight x value) / SUM(weight), where the weight is the number of people in your group in that city.

The national values are filled in (you can change them). The last table shows how much of your group lives in the 50 cities.

Notes

Cities: the 343 incorporated places with more than 75,000 residents in 2000, 2006-2010 and 2020-2024. Groups: the 59 countries and regions of birth with more than 75,000 members in the United States in 2000 that the Census Bureau reports the same way in all three periods. Sources: Census 2000 Summary File 3; American Community Survey 5-year estimates, 2006-2010 and 2020-2024. If two cities have exactly the same group size, the one with the lower city_code is ranked first.

Indicator definitions: poverty_percent is the percent of people below the poverty level; unemployment_percent is the percent of the civilian labor force; median_household_income is in 2024 dollars when the box on Start here is ticked (2000 and 2006-2010 restated with the annual consumer price index, CPI-U), and in the dollars of each period (1999, 2010, 2024) when it is not; bachelors_percent is the percent of adults 25 and over with a bachelor's degree or higher; renter_percent is the percent of occupied homes that are rented; black_percent is Black or African American alone (not Hispanic); hispanic_percent is Hispanic or Latino of any race. Each is the same Census table in all three periods. The weighted average of median_household_income is an average of city medians, not a median.

Check it yourself

Every number above comes from the Top 50 tab: the group sizes (the weights) and your variables. To calculate a weighted average yourself:

  1. Multiply each city's group size by its value, city by city.
  2. Add up those products (the "Sum of (group size x value)" row).
  3. Add up the group sizes (the "Sum of the group sizes" row).
  4. Divide the sum from step 2 by the sum from step 3.
