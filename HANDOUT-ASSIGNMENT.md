# Part 2: Where does the "average" member of your group live?

## The assignment

### The question

Crime is shaped by place. To explain crime among immigrants, you first need to know what the places where they live are like. **In what kind of city does the average member of your group live, and how does that city compare with the nation?**

### Length and format

**Your paper must be at least 4 pages of text.** Double-spaced, 12-point font, 1-inch margins. References and tables, figures and graphs do **not** count toward the 4 pages, so a paper with 3 pages of text and 2 pages of figures is too short.

### What you do

1. **Find your cities.** Use the 50 cities where your group is largest. The tool finds them for you (see the separate data instructions handout). It uses 343 cities with more than 75,000 residents in 2000, 2006-2010 and 2020-2024.
2. **Choose 3 indicators** from these 7: poverty percent, unemployment percent, median household income, bachelor's degree percent, renter percent, Black percent, Hispanic percent. Be ready to say why you chose them.
   - Use the **whole city's** value, not your group's. Example: the median income of *all* households in the city, not of your group's households.
3. **Calculate weighted averages.** For each indicator and each period, calculate:
   - **Weighted average** = sum of (group size x city value) / sum of group sizes. This is the **average member of your group**.
   - **National value.** Then compute *weighted average minus national* yourself.
4. **Make one figure or table per indicator**, showing all three periods. In the tool, **Make a figure** builds a titled chart, a table, or both for the indicator you choose, with the national values beside your weighted averages. Follow the formatting video and spreadsheet, and number each figure with a short caption.
5. **Write the profile** (it must be 4 pages of text, see Length and format). Draft with placeholders such as [Figure 1 about here] and add the figures last. This keeps the story flowing.

### How to write the comparison

> Members of this group lived in cities where XX.X% of people were below the poverty level, compared with XX.X% nationally. The average member's city was X.X points higher than the nation.

### Answer these in your text

1. What are conditions like in the average member's city, for each indicator?
2. How did they change across 2000, 2006-2010 and 2020-2024? Did the gap between the average member's city and the nation grow or shrink?
3. Does the group live in cities with high immigration in general? Did that change?
4. How does the national value help you read the average member's city? What would be missing without it?
5. What does a weighted average hide? Think about cities where the group is small.
6. A city line is political, not a neighborhood. What could differ inside these cities?
7. What share of your group lives in these 50 cities? What does that mean for how far your results describe the whole group?
